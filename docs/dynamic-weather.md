# Local weather and continuous storms

Normal native launches use the existing approximate IP location, without a location prompt. [Open-Meteo current model conditions](https://open-meteo.com/en/docs) supply cloud cover, rain/showers, wind, gusts, wind direction and thunderstorm codes. One small HTTPS request runs at most every 15 minutes, using the existing active-render maintenance check and wake events. There is no new polling timer. A saved response is reused after restart/offline; failures retain the current scene and back off for 15 minutes. Results older than a day are not reapplied from disk. Changing location rejects late replies from the previous area. This is approximate regional/model weather, not a measurement at the user's exact address.

The request explicitly selects metres/second and Unix timestamps. Rain/showers are converted from the API's current interval accumulation to mm/hour before mapping to optical density. The meteorological FROM bearing is converted into scene travel direction, taking the fixed geographic camera heading into account. Gusts contribute to the visual wind strength. No API key, GPS, user-entered coordinates, raw IP storage or additional service is required. Automated rendering never starts either location or weather requests.

## Three stages

1. **Severe storm:** dense overcast banks, storm-darkened cloud lighting, slanted foreground/distant rain, moving precipitation curtains, marine mist, increased whitecaps and large wind/swell waves. Sparse double-pulse lightning lights the rain and scene, with a distant channel. The hurricane preset targets 45 m/s visual wind over the independent background swell. Local rain no longer increases the swell or whitecap thresholds; see [the offshore sea-state audit](sea-state.md). It is an artistic severe-sea preset, not a hurricane forecast.
2. **Continuous strength:** the Weather menu offers Fair, Light Drizzle, Steady Rain, Thunderstorm and Hurricane, plus a continuous strength slider. Follow Local Weather returns to automatic conditions. Preview overrides do not stop periodic local updates. Rain, cloud amount, wind and thunder remain independent when driven by local data.
3. **Seamless changes:** weather targets feed an exact critically damped response that preserves current value and velocity when interrupted. Rain responds over seconds, cloud cover/wind over tens of seconds. Wind velocity is integrated into cloud coordinates, so a direction/speed change cannot teleport the cloud field. Existing cloud shape evolution continues throughout transitions. Fine water-texture drift also integrates wind direction; a month-uptime heading-change test guards against multiplying a new wind direction by the entire elapsed time.

The sea has a separate 90-second exponential response. A second deterministic complex spectrum is generated only when a new wind speed/direction target arrives (at most once a second during slider dragging). A bounded GPU pass relaxes current complex amplitudes toward it, retaining oscillator timing and the foam history. Existing waves grow and settle without reinitializing phases or changing their frequency. Retargeting partway through a storm starts from the current sea. Relaxation stops after 15 minutes when the residual is negligible. The second spectrum adds 6 MiB; no second ocean simulation, FFT, noise volume or display loop is created.

Rain is now an analytic world-space drop volume drawn into the existing HDR/MSAA scene with the actual ocean depth test. Perspective, projected shutter length, subpixel energy, wind-integrated travel and environment lighting vary its appearance. Waves hide drops behind/below their surface. Drop opacity fades with intensity without changing IDs. Distant rain shafts are integrated below the volumetric cloud base; the old screen-coordinate rain streaks, spray noise and sine-wave fog curtain have been removed from the composite shader. Lightning retains its separate rare atmospheric glow. Accelerated UTC fixtures advance clouds, weather response and the sea response on the same simulated timeline; normal operation uses ordinary time. The fixed camera never chases weather or celestial bodies.

## Verification

`npm run macos:weather:test` builds the app, tests response/cache/provider behavior offline, then runs native GPU storm, drizzle, lightning, night, portrait, buildup, settling, onset and long-clock fixtures under Metal API/shader validation. GPU readbacks include wave displacement, sky, reflection faces, sun visibility and display output. Tests assert zero windows, prohibited activation and no user-cache writes. A separate live HTTPS contract check used synthetic New York coordinates; routine tests never require network access.

See [the recorded rendering and timing report](weather-report-2026-10-30.json) and [current storm preview](storm-rework-preview.png). The wave-height check sums the six cascade height variances and reports four times their root-sum; it is a renderer proxy, not a local wave forecast or a complete wave-breaking simulation. Rain, cloud/fog, wind and thunder are mapped in this revision. Snow/hail particles, audio, real spray fluid dynamics and storm-surge modeling are outside these three rain/storm stages.

## Recorded performance

Sequential unprofiled runs on this Apple M1 used the same 1280×800 internal target and 30 fps cap, with 60 warmup frames excluded. Matched inactive-weather mean GPU time was **7.45 → 7.57 ms** before/after this feature. Current-app hurricane and building-sea workloads measured **7.24 ms** and **8.10 ms** mean, with P95 **9.70 ms** and **11.23 ms**, respectively. The storm workloads are additional workloads with different appearance, not equivalent-image comparisons. These are command-buffer timings, not battery life or whole-system energy measurements.

All 18 current weather GPU fixtures passed Metal API/shader validation. The rain-clock boundary changed mean display RGB by 4.98/255 over 20 ms, versus 4.85/255 over an adjacent 20 ms away from the boundary. Month-uptime directional detail phases advanced less than 0.00002 cycles per frame. The normal live preview fetched/cache-matched its IP location, and the slider/automatic-mode actions were verified in the native Weather menu.

## Rain and storm-formation rework

The [rework report](storm-rework-report-2026-10-30.json) and native recordings cover the visual revision after the initial rain overlay was rejected. Cloud banks now converge in a persistent weather field, develop greater vertical depth (1.25 → 6.25 km), lower their condensation base (1.5 → 1 km), expand/join their footprints, and retain internal billows and shadowed structure. Multiple-scattering fill and adaptive quadrature replace the harsh, undersampled bright rims. Fine erosion is filtered as banks deepen. High cirrus fade behind the storm deck. Heavy precipitation follows canopy formation; light drizzle stays available without deep convection.

`npm run macos:storm:review` produces a continuous accelerated buildup and a normal-speed rain recording from persistent native simulations, plus depth-disabled/no-drop diagnostic pairs. It requires development Python packages NumPy/Pillow and ffmpeg; the app itself has no such dependency. These recordings contain actual captured frames, with no synthetic crossfades or frame interpolation. Tests use no windows or network access. Normal weather fixtures and day/night/cloud regressions remain separate.

The current rework measured **10.07 ms** mean GPU frame time / **22.84 ms** P95 for the settled storm, versus **7.19 / 9.61 ms** for the prior overlay storm. A forming storm measured **9.05 / 14.92 ms**. These sequential runs use the same 1280×800 target and 30 fps cap; appearance intentionally changes. The additional draw remains inside the existing HDR/MSAA pass and allocates 64 bytes of uniform state, with no particle buffer or additional texture.

Review the [accelerated native buildup](storm-formation.mp4) and [normal-speed 30 fps rainfall](storm-rain-motion.mp4). The depth-disabled comparison identifies 10,446 ocean-region pixels where waves correctly hide rain; visible drop pixels vary with actual perspective and depth. Upper-sky opaque transmission coverage grows from about 53% to 100% in the recorded buildup. Those measurements establish occlusion and cloud formation, not photographic realism by themselves.
